On a typical flow the two models are barely half a second apart, 10.41 s against 9.81 s. The difference sits in the tail. Qwen2.5 3B had a few flows that ran far longer than the rest, one close to 20 seconds, and those pull its slowest 5% out to 12.99 s. Llama 3.2 3B never went past 11.2 s. For a batch job that runs overnight, the extra seconds disappear into the total. For an analyst waiting on one verdict at a time, the long Qwen flows are the ones that would be noticed.

A single RTX 3090 clears about six flows a minute with either model. The simulation sends it sixty, the default load in the app, so the queue only ever grows, and after an hour more than three thousand flows are still waiting. At that volume the choice of model hardly matters. What matters is the number of GPUs, about ten for either one, or cutting the volume first, for example by letting an intrusion detection system pass on only the flows it flags.

Qwen2.5 3B took 36 seconds on its first flow, four times a normal one, while Llama 3.2 3B started at nearly full speed. It is a one-off cost, paid every time the model is loaded. A service that keeps the model in memory pays it once and never again. One that loads the model on demand, or restarts often, pays it on every start, and for Qwen that is half a minute of waiting before the first answer.

Both pipelines spend their time the same way, which says the split comes from the job each agent is given rather than from the model. Reason, the one agent asked to explain its thinking, takes close to 40% of every flow. Decide only picks a label and uses a quarter of a second. Any work to speed up the pipeline should start with Reason, because a second saved there is worth more than everything Decide does.

Reason is running close to its limit. Five times in this session an answer reached the 200-token budget and was cut off, all of them from Qwen2.5 3B, which tends to write about 10% more than Llama in this step. That is also where its slowest flows came from. A larger Reason budget would let those answers finish, at the price of longer flows. A smaller one would save time and cut off more answers. Decide, by contrast, uses a quarter of its 12-token budget and could be cut without effect.

At the $0.256 an hour paid for this GPU, a thousand flows cost 77 cents on Qwen2.5 3B and 70 cents on Llama 3.2 3B. The gap comes entirely from speed, since the GPU is billed by the hour whatever it is doing. The energy figures point the same way. Both models drew about the same power, so the one that finishes sooner uses slightly less per flow. At ten thousand flows a day the difference is around seventy cents, small next to the cost of keeping enough GPUs to handle that volume in the first place.
